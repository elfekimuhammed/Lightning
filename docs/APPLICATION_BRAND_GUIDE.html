App guideline

2.4 · Meadowlark · updated 2026-10-01. How every screen looks, reads and adds up. The written rules are in APPLICATION_BRAND_GUIDE.md; this page shows each one. Built by docs/build_brand_guide.py from the same values as style.css. Sample figures are illustrative. Light theme only; dark is not wired in yet.

01

Seven rules behind every screen

  1. Lead on the left. The most important card of a section sits on the left, on the soft lead gradient.
  2. Support on the right. A white card beside it explains or extends the lead with a list, bars or a breakdown.
  3. Position, then activity. What you have on a date comes before what changed in a period. The period control sits only above what it changes.
  4. One name per number. A figure has one name and one home page (the Glossary). Other pages show it once and link there.
  5. Three control sizes. 48px for page controls, 36px inside cards and for chips, 30px for fields inside a register row. Nothing else.
  6. Colour means something. Green in and growing, soft rose out, azure held, strong rose needs you. Asset classes keep their own colours.
  7. Honest numbers. Estimates say “estimate”. Nothing is annualised from under a year or compared with an empty period.

Sections and pages

A section groups the cards that share one date: a line on the canvas, one pair of cards under it, 48px of canvas before the next.

  • Title left in sentence case; its date right (“As of” for position; the range under the title for activity).
  • One hairline 12px under the text and 16px above the cards; lead + support in 6 + 6 columns with 16px between, or one wide card.
  • A page has two to four sections, answers one question, and never repeats a number from another section.
  • Full pages opened from somewhere get one Back button in the header; popups get a full-page button beside close.
  • On phones the line wraps, the pair stacks lead first, tabs scroll on one line.

  • Primary (Nile) once per page; Secondary for the second action and every “Review”; Quiet for card-header actions and “Show more”; Danger only for deleting or deactivating.
  • Labels start with a verb, one to three words, no arrows. A page header holds Primary + Secondary + ⋯ at most.

09

Charts

Hand-drawn SVG and CSS, no chart library. Pick the form from the job the data does (over time, rank, parts, build-up, progress, spread); sometimes the answer is a stat card, not a chart.

Colour roles

Money inMoney outOver planMoney you holdPlanReference

Asset classes use their own colours (section 15). Sequential data uses one hue light to dark; diverging data two hues with a neutral centre.

Marks

2px lines, 10px dots with a 2px white ring, 12px bars with a rounded data end, 2px seams between stacked parts, recessive hairline grid, one axis, from zero.

Labels

On the marks, not in a legend, when they fit; the last value only on trends. Months read 2026-09. Tooltips on every dot and bar, and a “Show the numbers” table under every trend.

Honesty

Under two points says “Not enough history yet”. Missing data stays missing. Negative values never produce negative sizes or vanish.

Never

Two y-axesTwo scales on one chart invite false comparisons. Use two charts or index both to 100.
3D, shadows or gradients inside marksThey distort size and add nothing.
Gauges and speedometersA lot of ink for one number. Use a meter or a stat card.

Pies, and donuts over six slicesFold the rest into Other, or use bars.
Sunburst and funnelHard to read and nothing in personal finance needs them. Use grouped bars.
Rainbow palettes and cycled coloursColour follows meaning or the asset class, never the position.
A number on every pointLabel the last value and the ones that matter; the table has the rest.
Charts for empty or one-month dataSay what's missing instead.

Accessibility

  • Text contrast 4.5:1 on its ground; borders, icons and the focus ring 3:1.
  • Colour is never the only signal: amounts carry signs, states carry words, chart series carry labels or a legend.
  • Targets 44px or more (row fields sit in a 36px+ row). Esc closes popups and menus; disclosures work from the keyboard and report open or closed.
  • Every chart has an aria-label and a table view. Reduced motion turns transitions off.
13

Icons and logo

Lightning
  • Lucide icons at 1.75 stroke, 20px, in the current colour; no emoji or icon fonts. Key-note icons: check (good), bulb (info), alert circle (needs you).
  • The bolt sits beside the wordmark and is never recoloured by hand.
14

Before a screen ships, it has none of these

  • All-caps labels, including in the sidebar.
  • A vivid gradient card, or two leads in one section.
  • More than five table columns, or an empty table with headers.
  • Strong rose or green text for everyday amounts, or grey spending bars.
  • The lead on the right, or stretched to its neighbour’s height; white panels inside a lead card.
  • Text links with arrows (“Review →”); dropdowns for your own data.
  • White, bordered fields in a register row; a fourth control height.
  • Dates like “30 Sep 2026”; “– 5,000.00” or “-455.00”.
  • A period control above figures it doesn’t change; the same number under two names.
  • A chart from the Never list, or a chart for data under two points.

One hue family per kind of asset, a lighter shade for the fund version (--class-* in style.css). The order is the donut order and passes the palette validator for neighbouring slices. Some shades are under 3:1 on white, so charts always keep visible labels.
